# QCLT qubit audit

## What is being counted

`tdrjmcmc_qclt.py` and `qclt_amr.py` run classical Metropolis samplers. Their
QCLT moves draw the same distribution as the circuit in `qcov.py`, using
classical random choices. The reported circuit widths count **one kick**, not
an entire sampler with wavelets, temperatures, likelihood, and acceptance.

For one five-parameter wavelet, `K_RES = 6` and there are 15 history pairs.
The circuit represented in `cov_methods.py` uses six history vectors, five
signed output registers, and two independent coins per pair:

| Register purpose | 16-bit QCLT | 6-bit QCLT + AMR |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Six stored histories, five coordinates each | 480 | 180 |
| Five signed outputs | 105 | 55 |
| Fifteen move and fifteen sign coins | 30 | 30 |
| Standalone pair kick | **615** | **265** |
| Isotropic lattice coins, if included coherently | 10 | 10 |
| Complete proposal distribution | **625** | **275** |

The 610/260 numbers in `figures/previous/README.md` use output registers one bit
shorter than the current `qclt_amr.out_bits` helper and circuit construction.
With six histories, the extra signed bit can matter: a coordinate's maximum
pair sum can exceed the positive range of a 20-bit or 10-bit signed register.

## Exact reduction when the history is classical

In the production samplers, history is a NumPy array, so all 15 pair
differences are already known before a proposal is drawn. `compact_qclt.py`
uses each difference as a gate constant. It allocates no quantum history
registers and still accumulates the same signed step coherently until final
measurement. It includes the isotropic lattice term and uses the smallest
signed output width justified by the actual differences:

`width_p = 1 + bit_length(sum_pairs |difference_pair,p| + 1_if_isotropic)`.

| One wavelet kick, including isotropic term | Full 16-bit grid | AMR 6-bit window |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Output registers, worst-case width | 105 | 55 |
| Pair and isotropic coins | 40 | 40 |
| **Compact circuit, worst case** | **145** | **95** |

The full-grid circuit can be smaller for a particular narrow reservoir. It
fits below 100 exactly when the sum of its five output widths is at most 59
(assuming all 15 pairs are nonzero and both move probabilities lie strictly
between 0 and 1). Zero pairs and probabilities of 0 or 1 remove more coins.
The AMR circuit is at most 95 for six 6-bit histories, with no approximation.
If the isotropic term stays classical as it is in the production sampler, the
AMR QCLT circuit itself is at most 85 qubits. The 432-qubit, four-history
validation example in `tdrjmcmc_qclt.py` compiles to 89 qubits when its
isotropic term is included. For the 300 seeded eight-sample datasets described
by `cov_methods.py`, the analogous compact pair kick ranges from 87 to 96
qubits (median 93) by the register-width formula, versus the original
266-qubit construction.

The circuit is not free: a worst-case AMR example has 5,425 high-level gates
at 95 qubits, versus 9,010 at 275 qubits for the history-loaded equivalent.
The gate constants must change when the history or AMR window changes.
These are logical Qiskit widths. Multi-controlled additions can have large
decomposed depth, and using work qubits to reduce that depth would raise the
physical width. Ideal output-law equality does not establish fidelity on
noisy hardware.

Measuring just the 30 pair coins and 10 isotropic coins, then computing the
step classically, needs at most 40 qubits and gives the same proposal law. The
current sampler already does the equivalent entirely in classical code. This
40-qubit version does not produce a coherent step register.

These counts must not be read as a sub-100-qubit implementation of the whole
MCMC. A direct quantum register for one five-parameter wavelet on the 16-bit
base grid is already 80 qubits. Ten simultaneously represented wavelets would
use 800 before any proposal, likelihood, acceptance, or tempering register.
Even one 80-qubit wavelet updated in place with 40 fresh proposal coins would
reach 120. The under-100 result is for a standalone proposal whose output is
measured and then used by the existing classical sampler.

## Exact reduction for a coherent walk generated by known coins

The focused [QCLT+AMR walk study](../figures/quovar/README.md) has a useful
special structure. Its initial position and preparatory displacement modes
are fixed. Every history position is therefore a deterministic function of
the still-coherent preparatory sign coins. The pairwise QCLT differences are
computed directly from those coins, using one parity scratch qubit that is
uncomputed after each term. No history snapshot registers are needed, and
the adaptive kick still depends coherently on the history branch. The
compressed 29-qubit circuit was checked sample for sample against an
independent 64-qubit circuit that stores all three history snapshots. The
diagnostic displacement tracker adds 16 qubits solely to read an unwrapped
step.

For `d` coordinates, `K` preparatory snapshots, `b` bits per coordinate,
one covariance kick, and `d` known displacement modes, this construction's
operational width is

`d*b + K*d + K*(K-1) + 2*d + 1`.

The terms are current position, preparatory signs, QCLT pair coins,
isotropic coins, and parity scratch. The two-dimensional, three-history,
6-bit walk uses **29** qubits; its 16-bit full-range counterpart uses **49**.
With six histories in five dimensions on a 6-bit grid, the formula gives
**101** qubits including the isotropic floor, or **91** without that floor.
A 5-bit version with the floor would use 96, but this study does not establish
that five bits preserve accuracy in the five-dimensional application.

The reduction works because these histories were generated by known
reversible steps. An arbitrary unknown quantum history, or a history from a
branch-dependent likelihood and accept/reject process, cannot generally be
replaced by only these sign coins. Recomputing such a history may trade
register width for large reversible circuit depth. The AMR window in the
focused study is chosen classically from measurements between coherent walk
circuits; it is not a coherent in-circuit AMR update.

## Fully coherent APT + TD + RJ + MCMC feasibility

For a chain with exactly `k` wavelets, each on a `b`-bit grid in five
parameters, there are approximately `binomial(2^(5b), k)` distinct *unordered*
configurations. Therefore even an optimal encoding of the configuration alone
needs approximately `5bk - log2(k!)` qubits. This already gives:

| Figure setting | One chain, 16-bit state | Eight chains, 16-bit state | One chain, hypothetical 6-bit state | Eight chains, hypothetical 6-bit state |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Easy, up to 10 wavelets | 779 | 6,226 | 279 | 2,226 |
| Hard, up to 20 wavelets | 1,539 | 12,312 | 539 | 4,312 |

These are information lower bounds, rounded up, before active-count registers,
history, proposals, acceptance, likelihood, AMR windows, temperatures, or
fresh random choices. The production AMR sampler keeps its *state* on the
16-bit grid; only its kick uses 6-bit coordinates. Tagged superpositions do
not compress independent configurations below these bounds. The counts use
`binomial(2^(5b), k)`, which excludes duplicate wavelets and is therefore a
conservative bound for a model that permits them.

The `qaptdrjmcmc.ipynb` circuit is not a complete implementation of the
plotted algorithm. It has two chains, one history copy, a placeholder
likelihood that does not compute the waveform residual, no Metropolis
acceptance test, no birth/death or reversible-jump mapping, no adaptive
temperature ladder, and no AMR update. It performs one proposal-like block
and one uncontrolled swap-coin block before measurement. Its 230-qubit example
therefore cannot be scaled or used to regenerate the current plots as a
fully coherent quantum sampler.

`qcov.build_adaptive_walk` is a coherent proposal demonstration, but does not
implement the missing MCMC components. Its fresh coins and snapshots grow with
the number of steps. A complete measurement-free realization would need a
new reversible likelihood/acceptance construction and a way to update
branch-dependent adaptation coherently. The existing PDF results are from
classical MCMC with an exactly simulated QCLT proposal, so relabeling or
redrawing them would not establish performance of that new algorithm.

## Why a tagged superposition does not replace two working values

`(|psi_0>|0> + |psi_1>|1>)/sqrt(2)` occupies one state register and one tag
qubit. It gives access to one tagged alternative in each branch. The pair
difference operation needs both values in the same branch. If the values are
classical, a lookup circuit can load them from an address, trading qubits for
state-preparation gates; compiling their differences as constants is simpler
here. If the two states are unknown quantum inputs, the tagged expression is
not a lossless replacement for two independently available registers.

## Code review relevant to the qubit target

- `qcov.py` implements a reversible pair-difference walk with fresh coins and
  no intermediate measurement. Its history-loaded layout is appropriate for
  *coherent quantum history*, but costly when the history is already classical.
  Reusing its coins without reset would correlate successive choices.
- `qcov.build_adaptive_walk` is a separate fully coherent demonstration. It
  adds history snapshots and new coins over time. The figures' kick counts do
  not describe this growing circuit.
- `tdrjmcmc_qclt.py` uses six classical reservoir samples per time-frequency
  cell. This is the minimum sample count that can give a full-rank covariance
  in five dimensions. The QCLT kernel is symmetric, but differs from the
  classical AP Gaussian because the six-sample covariance is noisy, its coin
  law is discrete, and its move probability is capped at one.
- Its full-grid QCLT proposal takes raw phase differences across the periodic
  seam, while the generic `qclt_amr.py` sampler takes minimal-image periodic
  differences. This can cause unnecessarily large phase kicks near the seam.
  The compact circuit accepts either convention through its input differences;
  this audit preserves each sampler's existing convention.
- `tdrjmcmc_qclt.py` calculates the isotropic term, AMR principal axes,
  coordinate transform, and Metropolis decision classically. A full quantum
  implementation would need additional reversible arithmetic and likelihood
  logic; the 95-qubit count does not include them.
- AMR clips stored history points that lie outside its moving window and
  rejects proposals that leave the window. Those choices can affect mixing
  and accuracy. The compact circuit changes neither choice.
- `qclt_amr.py` uses `K = 2d` for generic applications, rather than the
  wavelet sampler's `K = d+1`. At `d=5`, `K=10` has 45 pairs: its 90 pair
  coins plus 10 isotropic coins already reach 100 before any output register.
  Reducing `K` may change proposal quality, so this audit leaves it alone.
- `cov_methods.py` compares covariance-estimation circuits with a QCLT
  *step-generating* circuit. The comparison is informative, but the smaller
  estimators measure an estimate and draw a step classically; the workloads
  and omitted state-preparation costs differ.
- The original `FINAL.ipynb` computes covariance and its matrix square root
  classically. Its small quantum walk primitive is therefore a different
  resource question from computing a history-dependent kick coherently.
- The original `qapthtdrjmcmc.ipynb` shows a 10-data-qubit, 5-coin-qubit
  walk on four-point parameter grids. It reuses those coins across two steps;
  this does not give two independent random steps. On its four-point grid,
  the chosen `+2` and `-2` moves also coincide for four of the five parameters.
- The original `qaptdrjmcmc.ipynb` allocates 230 qubits for five wavelets at
  three bits per parameter: two chains plus one history copy already account
  for 225. Its likelihood circuit is marked as a placeholder, so that width
  is not evidence that a complete coherent sampler fits at that scale.

`test_compact_qclt.py` compares the new circuit against independent exact
enumeration and the old history-loaded circuit, including the isotropic term.
The proposal law is identical; posterior accuracy is therefore unchanged for
the same classical history and acceptance rule. No MCMC result or figure was
regenerated by this audit.
